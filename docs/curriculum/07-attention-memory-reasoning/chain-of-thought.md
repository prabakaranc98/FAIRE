## What it is

Imagine you are trying to solve a complex math problem, and a friend shows you their solution, step-by-step. Chain of Thought (CoT) prompting applies this same principle to large language models (LLMs). By providing a series of intermediate reasoning steps, CoT encourages models to break down complex tasks into smaller, manageable sub-problems. This approach significantly improves performance on reasoning-intensive tasks where a direct answer is difficult to derive.

In standard prompting, a model attempts to map an input directly to an output in a single forward pass. When the task is complex—what we might call a monolithic task that requires multiple logical dependencies—the model often fails because it lacks the "scratchpad" space to process intermediate variables. By generating a sequence of intermediate tokens, the model effectively uses its own output as an external memory, allowing the transformer's attention mechanism to attend to previous logical steps rather than just the initial prompt.

## Why it matters at the frontier

CoT bridges the gap between simple pattern matching and genuine logical inference. By manipulating the prompt to elicit reasoning, researchers can unlock capabilities in existing models without requiring expensive fine-tuning or architectural changes. This makes it a primary tool for practitioners looking to improve model performance on domain-specific reasoning tasks.

The field is currently grappling with the diminishing value of CoT in newer, more capable models. As models become better at implicit reasoning, the overhead of generating long chains of text—in terms of both latency and token cost—becomes a critical bottleneck. The central challenge for frontier labs today is balancing these accuracy gains against computational efficiency.

## Core concepts

- **CoT Prompting** — A technique where a model is prompted with examples of step-by-step reasoning to elicit similar behavior on new inputs.
- **Zero-Shot CoT** — The practice of appending a phrase like "Let's think step by step" to a prompt to trigger reasoning without providing explicit examples.
- **Reasoning Trace** — The sequence of intermediate tokens generated by the model that constitutes its logical path toward a final answer.
- **Decomposition** — The process of breaking a complex problem into smaller, sequential sub-tasks that are easier for the model to solve.
- **Token Overhead** — The additional computational cost and latency incurred by generating long reasoning chains during inference.

## Mathematical foundations

While CoT is primarily an empirical prompting technique, it can be framed as a search over a latent reasoning space.

\[
P(y \mid x) = \sum_{z \in \mathcal{Z}} P(y \mid z, x) P(z \mid x)
\]
where \(y\) is the final answer, \(x\) is the input prompt, \(z\) is the intermediate reasoning trace, and \(\mathcal{Z}\) is the set of all possible reasoning paths.

\[
\mathcal{L}_{CoT} = -\log P(z^*, y^* \mid x)
\]
where \(z^*\) is the optimal reasoning trace and \(y^*\) is the ground truth answer, penalizing models that fail to generate the correct reasoning steps.

## Key algorithms / techniques

- **Few-Shot CoT** — Providing the model with several input-output pairs that include reasoning steps to guide the model's output format.
- **Zero-Shot CoT** — Using a simple trigger phrase to induce reasoning, which is useful when labeled examples are unavailable.
- **Chain-of-Draft** — A technique that generates a "draft" of the reasoning process to prune irrelevant information before the final output, reducing token usage.

## Current SotA

Reasoning performance is currently dominated by models trained with reinforcement learning on reasoning traces, such as OpenAI's o1 series. According to the OpenAI o1 System Card (2024), o1 achieves 83.3% on the MATH benchmark, significantly outperforming standard GPT-4o (approx. 60% with CoT). This shift suggests that the design choice of "training for reasoning" is replacing "prompting for reasoning."

## What's happening now

Research is shifting from static prompting to dynamic reasoning control. Zhu et al. (2025) have proposed frameworks for "controlling" the reasoning process, moving away from simple prompting toward models that can self-correct or adjust their reasoning depth based on task difficulty.

Engineering efforts are focused on mitigating the latency of CoT. Techniques like Chain-of-Draft aim to reduce the token count of reasoning traces while maintaining accuracy. This is critical for production systems where every token generated increases inference cost and latency.

The open problem remains the lack of an automated, adaptive strategy for CoT. As noted in the Prompting Science Report 2 (2025), current methods rely on manual prompt engineering or trial-and-error, which is unsustainable as models grow in complexity.

## Open questions

> **Researcher:** How can we formalize the "reasoning space" \(\mathcal{Z}\) to enable differentiable optimization of reasoning traces rather than relying on heuristic prompting?

> **Engineer:** Can we implement a lightweight "reasoning-gate" on consumer hardware (e.g., RTX 3090) that decides whether to trigger a CoT path based on input complexity?

> **Open:** Does the emergence of reasoning in models trained via RL render the "prompting" aspect of CoT obsolete, or will prompting remain a necessary interface for human-model collaboration?
